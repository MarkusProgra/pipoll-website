Build Your Mission. Everything Else Will Follow.

Why the Men Who Chase Women Rarely Become Great

By Markus & Oliver July 10, 2026 6 min read

"The moment you put women above your purpose is the moment you begin to lose both."

There is a lie that almost every young man is taught.

Not in school.

Not by his parents.

But by the world.

Open social media and you'll see it everywhere.

A Lamborghini.

A luxury watch.

An attractive woman.

Millions of followers.

The message is always the same:

This is success. Chase it.

The problem isn't that these things are bad.

The problem is that they are terrible goals.

The Shadow Problem

Imagine trying to catch your own shadow.

The faster you run after it, the farther away it seems.

Now turn around and walk toward the sun.

Without even trying, your shadow follows.

Life works the same way.

Money.

Status.

Recognition.

Cars.

Even relationships.

Most of them are shadows.

Purpose is the sun.

When you chase the shadows, you often lose your purpose—and still fail to catch what you wanted.

When you chase your purpose, many of the shadows begin following naturally.

The World's Definition of Success Is Surprisingly Small

Think about what society calls success.

Own a nicer car than your neighbor.

Date a more attractive woman.

Wear a more expensive watch.

Get more likes than everyone else.

Really?

You were given one life.

One mind.

One unique combination of gifts that has never existed before and never will again.

Humanity has built civilizations, cured diseases, explored space, written masterpieces, and created technologies that transformed billions of lives.

And somehow we've convinced ourselves that success is parking a more expensive piece of metal in the driveway.

A supercar is incredible.

I hope one day you own your dream car.

But after the first week...

Then what?

Will you finally be complete because you own carbon fiber and aluminum?

Will your purpose suddenly appear because the engine sounds better?

Of course not.

A car should be a souvenir of your journey—not the destination.

Lust Doesn't Just Attack Your Purity.
It Attacks Your Purpose.

The Bible never warns against lust because attraction is evil.

It warns against lust because misplaced desire has the power to control your life.

"Each person is tempted when they are dragged away by their own desire and enticed." — James 1:14

The devil rarely destroys a man overnight.

He distracts him.

If he can convince a man that his highest purpose is female attention, pleasure, status, or approval, then he doesn't have to destroy his potential.

The man does it himself.

Instead of asking,

"What problem can I solve?"

he asks,

"How do I impress people?"

Instead of asking,

"How can I serve God with the gifts He gave me?"

he asks,

"How do I get more attention?"

The mission quietly disappears.

Psychology Says the Same Thing

Modern psychology makes an important distinction between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation.

Intrinsic motivation comes from purpose, mastery, growth, contribution, and meaningful work.

Extrinsic motivation comes from status, approval, appearance, wealth, and external rewards.

Study after study has shown that people whose lives revolve around intrinsic goals experience greater long-term well-being, persistence, and fulfillment.

Why?

Because external rewards never stay satisfying.

Psychologists call this hedonic adaptation.

You buy the dream car.

Soon it becomes your normal car.

You gain attention.

Soon you need more.

You reach one milestone.

Your mind immediately invents another.

The finish line keeps moving.

Purpose doesn't.

The Most Dangerous Man Is Not the Strongest.
The Most Dangerous Man Is the One Who Cannot Be Bought.

Think about history.

The hardest people to manipulate were never those with the biggest muscles.

They were the ones whose principles were greater than their desires.

Now think about the opposite.

A man whose highest goal is women.

A man who lives for validation.

A man who craves attention.

How difficult is he to manipulate?

Not difficult at all.

Offer him pleasure.

Offer him approval.

Offer him temptation.

He will trade away discipline.

Then integrity.

Then purpose.

A man whose desires are stronger than his principles is one of the easiest people in the world to control.

That is why lust is so dangerous.

It doesn't merely affect morality.

It hands someone else the steering wheel to your life.

Women Are Not the Goal

Women matter.

Marriage matters.

Love matters.

They are beautiful gifts from God.

But gifts make terrible gods.

There is an enormous difference between loving someone and building your identity around their approval.

Healthy relationships grow alongside purpose.

They should never replace it.

Ironically, the men who spend their lives chasing women usually struggle to attract healthy relationships.

The men who devote themselves to becoming disciplined, competent, kind, courageous, and purpose-driven often attract them naturally.

Because confidence cannot be faked.

Purpose cannot be faked.

Character cannot be faked.

Look at the Men God Used

Joseph refused temptation when Potiphar's wife pursued him.

He lost comfort but preserved his character.

David's greatest victories happened while following God.

His greatest failure began when he followed lust.

Jesus Himself rejected power, comfort, popularity, and immediate gratification whenever they conflicted with His mission.

There is a pattern throughout Scripture.

God uses men who know what they are living for.

The Goals Worth Chasing

Everyone wants money.

Everyone wants attractive partners.

Everyone wants expensive cars.

Everyone wants recognition.

There is nothing exceptional about wanting what everyone else wants.

Average desires produce average lives.

The people who truly change the world pursue something much bigger.

They build companies that improve lives.

They create technology that solves real problems.

They heal.

They teach.

They discover.

They create.

They lead.

They serve.

They leave humanity better than they found it.

Those are goals worthy of your life.

Everything else is decoration.

Invest in the Person You're Becoming

The greatest investment you'll ever make isn't in a stock portfolio or a luxury purchase.

It's in your character.

In your discipline.

In your mission.

That's also the philosophy behind People—an app built around the idea that the most valuable investment isn't someone's possessions, but the trajectory of their life and the goals they're committed to achieving. A meaningful future has always been worth more than a polished image.

The Great Paradox

Here's what almost nobody tells young men.

Stop chasing women.

Stop chasing status.

Stop chasing approval.

Stop chasing the image of success.

Instead...

Become the kind of man who cannot help but create value.

Become the man who wakes up with a mission.

The man who serves.

The man who builds.

The man who solves difficult problems.

The man who keeps his word when no one is watching.

Ironically, many of the things you once chased begin following you.

Not because you wanted them.

But because they were never supposed to be the goal.

One Final Thought

One day your car will belong to someone else.

Your money will be spent.

Your appearance will fade.

Even your name may eventually be forgotten.

But every person you inspired...

Every problem you solved...

Every family you strengthened...

Every act of courage...

Every sacrifice you made for something greater than yourself...

Those things echo long after you're gone.

So don't build a life that merely looks successful.

Build one that was worth living.

Because the moment you place women above your purpose, you begin to lose both.

But when you place God, purpose, and service above everything else...

You often discover that everything you once chased was never worth chasing in the first place.

Invest in the person you're becoming.
